# Children's access assessment (draft)

Date: 2026-11-12. Status: draft for owner and legal review. Next review: by
2027-11-12, or sooner (see "Re-assess early" below).

The Online Safety Act asks two questions. A service must assume children can
access it unless it uses **highly effective age assurance**. A tick box or a
self-declared date of birth is not enough on its own, so this assessment does
**not** assume the 18+ checks keep children out.

## Stage 1: Is it possible for children to access the service?

**Yes.** The service relies on a date of birth that users enter themselves and
a tick box. Ofcom says self-declaration and terms that ban children are not
highly effective age assurance. So the service can't conclude children are
unable to access it, and Stage 2 applies.

## Stage 2: Is the child user condition met?

Is there a significant number of children using the service, or is it likely
to attract a significant number?

**Evidence it is not met:**

- Built for adult race entrants; only adult race categories exist (no junior
  categories).
- Promoted to people who have bought tickets for adult events (first customer:
  KeltX's adult doubles races).
- Date of birth required before use; under-18 dates are refused by the
  database and can't be changed afterwards.
- Terms state 18+ only; suspected under-18 reports are urgent, and the account
  is suspended while checked.
- Very small (about 100 users expected).
- No under-18 users found to date (2026-11-12: no reports of this kind).

**Evidence it could be met:**

- Fitness racing appeals to teenagers, and the organiser also runs junior
  races (for 12 to 15 year olds).
- Ofcom says a "significant" number can be small in absolute terms.
- No independent age check.

## Conclusion (draft)

The child user condition is **probably not met, with low confidence**.

So the children's risk assessment and children's safety duties are not
treated as applying for now. This conclusion must be confirmed by a solicitor.

## Monitoring

- Every "suspected under 18" report, and its outcome, is recorded in the
  moderation screen.
- Count these at each review.

## Re-assess early when

- any under-18 user is found;
- the service is marketed to wider audiences (for example junior events,
  schools, social media aimed at teenagers);
- the organiser asks for junior categories;
- a solicitor disagrees with the conclusion.

If the condition is met, the children's risk assessment and safety duties
apply, and age assurance (for example facial age estimation) should be
reconsidered. Any paid or ID-based check needs the owner's approval.
